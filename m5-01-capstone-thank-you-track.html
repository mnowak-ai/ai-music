🏆 Capstone Project

The Thank You Track

One song. One person who believed in you before you had given them a reason to. This is everything you have learned in this course, pointed at something that actually matters.

Project time: 45–60 min Tool: Suno (free account) Deliverable: one finished song
01

The Assignment

Make a song for someone who had an impact on your life, and give it to them.

That is the whole thing. Not a song about them for an audience. A song for them, that they will hear.

The person might be a teacher who stayed late. A parent or guardian who worked doubles. A grandparent who kept showing up. A coach who put you in when nobody else would have. A mentor, a counselor, an older sibling, a boss who trained you when they did not have to, a neighbor, a friend who talked you out of quitting something.

You are going to use AI to make it, and that is the point. Fifteen years ago this assignment was impossible for anyone who was not already a musician. You will do it in under an hour, and the person on the other end will not care what software you used. They will care that you thought about them.

STEP 1
Choose your person
One person. Specific. Someone real who changed something for you.
STEP 2
Three things
Three specific things you want to thank them for. Specific is the whole game.
STEP 3
Generate lyrics
AI-assisted, using your details. Then you edit them, because they are yours.
STEP 4

Pick a genre and mood that fits them, not what you usually listen to.
STEP 5
Produce the track
Generate it in Suno, pick the best version, and deliver it.
02

The One Thing That Decides If This Works

Before you start, understand the single difference between a Thank You Track that makes someone cry and one that makes them say "aw, thanks."

Specificity.

"Thank you for always believing in me" is a nice sentence that could be handed to literally anyone. It will produce a nice song that could have been for anyone. Nobody has ever been moved by a sentence that could have been about them.

"You let me eat lunch in your classroom for a whole semester when I did not have anywhere else to sit" is about exactly one person, and only they know what it meant. That is what makes a song land.

03

Taking It Into Suno

You now have two prompts. Here is exactly what to do with them.

1
Get your lyrics written first. Paste your lyric prompt into an AI assistant, or into Suno's own lyrics helper. You will get a full set of lyrics back with your details in them.
2
Now edit those lyrics, and take this seriously. Read every line out loud. Cut anything that sounds like a greeting card. Fix anything that is not true. Put back the actual words you would use. This is the step that turns a generated song into your song, and it is the step being graded.
3
In Suno, switch to Custom mode. Paste your edited lyrics into the lyrics box, and paste your style prompt into the style box. Add a title.
4
Generate, then listen to every version it gives you. Suno usually returns more than one. They will differ, sometimes a lot. Pick the one that sounds like the feeling you were going for, not just the one that sounds most professional.
5
Regenerate if it misses. If the vocal is wrong or the energy is off, adjust your style prompt and run it again. Two or three tries is normal, not failure. Change one thing at a time so you learn what each word in the prompt actually does.
Practical heads-up on the free account. Suno's free tier is enough to make this song, but downloads and commercial use are restricted on free accounts, and the platform has been changing its terms as its licensing deals roll out. For a class project you do not need to download anything: you can share the song as a link. Check the current terms in the app before you assume anything, which is a good habit for every AI tool you will ever use.
04

Now the Hard Part: Giving It to Them

Most students finish the song and then freeze here, because sending it feels vulnerable in a way making it did not.

Do it anyway. A song nobody hears is a homework assignment. A song they hear is the actual point of this project, and I promise the awkwardness lasts about nine seconds.

💌 The Delivery Plan
Pick Your Approach
One rule for delivery. Do not oversell it and do not apologize for it. No "it's kind of dumb but," no "I made this for a class so don't read into it." You made something for someone on purpose. Let it be that. If you undercut it before they hear it, you have taught them how to receive it.
05

Be Honest About the AI

You spent this whole module learning that disclosure costs nothing and protects everything. Apply it here.

Tell them you used AI to make it. Not as an apology, as information. Something as simple as "I used an AI music tool to produce it, but the words and everything in them are mine" is complete, honest, and takes four seconds.

Here is why it matters more than usual in this specific case: this is a gift built on sincerity. Discovering later that part of it was hidden would undercut the one thing the song was for. And the truth is genuinely impressive anyway. You learned a new set of tools and used them to make something for someone. That is a better story than pretending you have secretly been a producer this whole time.

06

What Makes This Project Good

If this is being graded, or if you just want to know whether yours is any good, here is the standard.

What's assessed
What strong work looks like
Specificity
The three things are concrete moments, not general praise. Someone reading the lyrics could tell this was written for one particular person.
Human editing
The lyrics were meaningfully revised after generation. You can point to lines you changed and explain why.
Style fit
The genre and mood suit the person and the message, and you can explain the choice in a sentence.
Prompt craft
You iterated. You can describe what you changed between generations and what effect it had.
Ethics
AI use is disclosed. No real person's voice or likeness was cloned. The song is kind.
Delivery
The song actually reached the person, and you can say what happened when it did.
Notice what is not on that list: whether the song sounds like a hit. That is not the assignment, and it never was. The assignment is whether the person on the receiving end felt seen.
07

Last Thing

You started this course learning what the music industry is, and you are ending it having made something with the same tools working professionals are arguing about right now.

Along the way you learned what a master recording is and why artists fight for it. You built a beat, mixed a track, designed a cover, cut video to a beat, read a contract, and thought hard about what it means to use these tools without hurting anyone. That is not a small amount of ground.

But the reason this project is the capstone and not a quiz is that it puts the whole thing in the right order. The tools got easier. What they are for did not change. Someone believed in you. Now you can tell them, in a form they can play back whenever they want.

Go make it. Then actually send it.
