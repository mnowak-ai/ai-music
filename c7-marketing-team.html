01

The Job, In Real Life

Music marketers make sure that when a song drops, the world actually finds out. They run artists' social media, plan release campaigns, respond to fans, pitch songs to playlists, create promotional content, and increasingly, engineer the online moments that turn unknown songs into phenomena.

This career contains several jobs worth knowing by name. Social media managers run an artist's daily online presence. Publicists (the music world's public relations specialists) get artists covered by media: interviews, articles, and reviews. Digital marketers run advertising campaigns and analyze the data. Street teams and fan engagement roles build community directly with listeners.

Here's why this career deserves your attention: it's where the industry's power has moved. A generation ago, radio decided what got popular. Today, a marketing team that understands short-form video and fan communities can break a song globally in a week, and labels know it, which is why these teams keep growing. The BLS projects employment of public relations specialists to grow 5 percent from 2024 to 2034, faster than the average for all occupations.

How Do You Get This Job?

This career has a classic education path AND a famous shortcut, and smart candidates walk both at once.

🎓
Formal education
A bachelor's degree is the typical credential
The BLS lists a bachelor's degree as the typical entry-level education for public relations specialists, usually in marketing, communications, journalism, or business. Music business programs increasingly offer dedicated music marketing tracks.
🛤️
The famous shortcut
Receipts beat resumes
Music marketing is famous for hiring people who can prove they grow audiences. Students who run a fan account with 50,000 followers, promote school events that sell out, or grow a local artist's channels have receipts, and receipts open doors that degrees alone can't. The strongest applicants bring both.
💪
Skills that matter most
Platform fluency, writing, and data
Native fluency in social platforms and trends; sharp, fast writing (captions, pitches, and campaigns are all words); and data literacy, reading analytics to learn what's working. Add genuine love of fan communities, because faking it shows.

What this means: the median PR specialist earned $69,780 per year, and the field is growing faster than average (5 percent projected from 2024 to 2034, per the BLS). Entry-level music marketing coordinators typically start toward the lower end, while marketing directors and heads of digital at labels climb past the top of this bar. Freelance social media managers also commonly run several artist accounts at once, stacking monthly retainers.

The AI Angle

How AI Is Changing This Career

AI is now the marketing team's junior staff. Tools draft caption options, generate visual assets, schedule content at optimal times, analyze which posts drive streams, and predict which cities to advertise in. Publicists use AI to research outlets and personalize hundreds of pitches. What took a team a week now takes an afternoon.

The twist: because every team has these tools, the advantage shifts to judgment. Knowing which AI-drafted post actually sounds like the artist, sensing which trend fits the brand and which would embarrass it, and building real fan relationships, that's the human layer. In marketing, AI raises the floor; taste and authenticity raise the ceiling.
