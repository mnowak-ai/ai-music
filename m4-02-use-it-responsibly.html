Use It Responsibly

The last article was about your work. This one is about people, including you. Your face and your voice used to be things only you had. Now they are data, and data can be copied.

Reading time: 10 min Interactive: 2 exercises Level: Beginner
01

Four Rules, Two Directions

Everything in this article comes down to four rules. Two of them protect other people from you. Two of them protect you from other people.

🧑‍🎤
Be careful when sharing your image or likeness in music videos and cover art
🙋
Don't use someone's voice, or face, without asking them first
🎙️
Remember: AI can copy voices, so protect your own
🚫
Don't use music to make fun of, embarrass, or bully someone

None of this means hide. Musicians have to be visible, and a career in this industry is built partly on people seeing your face and hearing your voice. The goal is not to disappear. The goal is to be visible on purpose, and to know what you are handing over when you post.

02

What One Post Actually Gives Away

Here is the thing that surprises people. No single post is the problem. The problem is combinations. A clear photo of your face is one thing. A clear photo plus twenty seconds of your speaking voice plus your real full name is a different thing entirely.

    03

    Rule 3, Specifically: Your Voice

    Voice deserves its own section, because singers are unusually exposed and most people have not thought about it.

    Voice cloning tools have gotten good enough that a fairly short clean sample can produce a convincing imitation. For a singer, clean vocal samples are not rare, they are your entire public output. That is not a reason to stop posting your music. It is a reason to know two things.

    First, this is a real and growing problem, not a hypothetical one. The FBI's 2025 Internet Crime Report logged more than 22,000 AI-related fraud complaints with losses exceeding $893 million, and voice cloning is a significant part of that picture. Much of it is not aimed at musicians at all, it is aimed at their families: a cloned voice on a phone call asking a relative for money urgently.

    Second, protect the people around you, not just yourself. The single most effective thing you can do costs nothing: agree on a family code word. A word only your household knows, that anyone claiming an emergency has to say. It sounds almost too simple to work, which is exactly why it works, because a voice clone cannot know something it was never trained on.

    Do this one today. Pick a word with your family. Something random, not your dog's name or your street. If a call ever comes that sounds like you and asks for money or help urgently, the word is what settles it. Tell them the reason too, so nobody dismisses it as paranoid.

    05

    Rule 4: The Line Between a Joke and Harm

    Music has always roasted people. Diss tracks are a real tradition, comedy songs are a real genre, and nobody is asking you to be humorless.

    But AI changed the math in a specific way, and it is worth naming precisely. Before, making a convincing video or recording of someone saying something they never said took real skill and effort. Now it takes a few minutes. The effort barrier that used to stop most impulsive cruelty is gone.

    So here is the practical difference. A joke invites the other person to laugh. Harassment does not. If the content only works because the target is humiliated, if you would not show it to them first, if it puts words in their mouth they never said, or if you are making it because you are angry at them, it has stopped being a joke regardless of how funny your friends find it.

    And understand the stakes clearly: this category is where AI misuse stops being a school discipline issue and becomes a legal one.

    06

    Where the Law Actually Stands

    This area has changed fast, and most people have not caught up. A short, current picture:

    FEDERAL
    The TAKE IT DOWN Act, signed into law in May 2025, makes it a federal crime to knowingly publish non-consensual intimate images of adults or minors, and it expressly includes AI-generated "digital forgeries." Penalties reach two years in prison, and three when a minor is depicted.
    PLATFORMS
    That same law requires covered platforms to run a notice-and-removal process and to take reported content down within 48 hours of a valid request, plus make reasonable efforts to remove reposts. That platform requirement took effect in May 2026, so the process exists now.
    VOICE
    Tennessee's ELVIS Act was written specifically to protect musicians' voices from unauthorized AI cloning, and a growing list of states including California, Illinois, New York, Washington and others have laws restricting harmful or commercial use of a cloned voice or likeness.
    FRAUD
    The FCC has ruled that AI voice-clone robocalls are illegal, and the FTC's impersonation rule bars using cloned voices to pose as businesses or government agencies. Using a voice clone to trick someone out of money can also trigger ordinary fraud and identity theft laws in every state.

    Worth being precise about one gap, because you will hear it argued: the federal law is narrower than people assume. It targets non-consensual intimate imagery specifically. A non-intimate deepfake, say a fake video of a classmate saying something they never said, is generally handled by state law, platform rules, and school policy rather than that particular statute. That does not make it acceptable, and it does not make it consequence-free.

    07

    If This Happens to You

    If someone makes AI content of your face or voice without your permission, here is what to do. Read this now so you are not reading it for the first time in a bad moment.

    1
    Understand that it is not your fault. Not because of what you posted, not because of what you wore, not because you are a performer with a public voice. Someone else chose to do something wrong. Start from there, because shame is what keeps people from reporting.
    2
    Save evidence before anything else. Screenshots, links, URLs, usernames, dates, and the account that posted it. Content gets deleted and re-uploaded constantly, and having the record first makes every later step possible.
    3
    Report it on the platform. Look for impersonation, synthetic media, or non-consensual content in the reporting menu, since a correctly categorized report moves much faster than a generic one. For intimate content, the 48-hour takedown requirement now applies.
    4
    Tell a trusted adult, and do not do this alone. A parent, teacher, counselor, or coach. If you are a student, your school almost certainly has a policy covering this, and adults can escalate in ways you cannot. For serious cases, this is also worth reporting to law enforcement.
    5
    Know that free help exists. If you are under 18 and intimate images of you are circulating, the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children runs a free service called Take It Down that helps get such content removed from participating platforms. You do not have to hand your images to anyone to use it.
    And if you are the one who did it. Take it down immediately, apologize directly and without excuses, and tell an adult yourself before they find out another way. That is genuinely the best version of a bad situation, for you and for the person you hurt. Trying to hide it makes everything that follows worse.
    08

    Check Your Understanding

    QUESTION 1
    Why is a family code word an effective protection against voice cloning scams?
    Cloning copies sound, not knowledge. A shared secret is something the clone has no way to access, which is why such a simple step works so well.
    QUESTION 2
    Someone gives you clear permission to use their face in a video you are making to mock them. What does the Consent Check say?
    Intent matters alongside consent. A yes obtained under social pressure is also not real consent, which is why the intent question is a hard stop.
    QUESTION 3
    Under the TAKE IT DOWN Act, how quickly must a covered platform remove reported non-consensual intimate content after a valid request?
    48 hours, plus reasonable efforts to remove duplicates and reposts. The platform requirement took effect in May 2026.

    Key Takeaways

    01

    No single post is the risk. Combinations are. Face plus voice plus real name plus location unlocks far more than any one of them alone.

    02

    Singers are unusually exposed to voice cloning because clean vocal samples are their public output. A family code word is a free, genuinely effective protection.

    03

    Ask before using anyone's face or voice, get a parent's permission for minors, and remember that consent does not make humiliating content acceptable.

    04

    If it happens to you: it is not your fault, save evidence, report it on the platform, and tell a trusted adult. Real legal protections and free help now exist.

    This article is educational material, not legal advice. Laws covering AI likeness, voice, and synthetic media vary by state and are changing quickly. For a specific situation, talk to a trusted adult and, where appropriate, a qualified attorney or law enforcement.
