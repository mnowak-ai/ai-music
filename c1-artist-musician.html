🎤 Music Career Deep Dive

Artist / Musician

The face and voice of the music. It's the career everyone pictures first, and the one with the most myths around it. Let's look at what the job is really like.

Reading time: 7 min Interactive: 2 activities Level: Beginner
01

The Job, In Real Life

An artist or musician writes, sings, and performs music. That's the simple version. The honest version: being an artist is really three jobs stacked together. You're a creator (writing and recording songs), a performer (playing live shows), and a small business (promoting yourself, building fans, and managing money).

Here's something most people don't know: the majority of working musicians are not world-famous stars. They're wedding band players, church musicians, session players who record parts for other people's songs, touring backup singers, and independent artists releasing music from home studios. According to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS), there are about 169,800 musician and singer jobs in America, and part-time work is common. Fame is rare. Working in music is not.

One more myth to clear up: you don't wait to be discovered anymore. Today's artists build their own audiences online, release music themselves through distributors, and treat every post, show, and song as a step in growing something real.

How Do You Get This Job?

Here's the surprising truth about this career: it has one of the most open doors in the whole industry.

🎓
Formal education
No degree required for most paths
According to the BLS, musicians and singers typically need no formal educational credential to enter the occupation. The big exception: classical music and opera, where artists often earn a bachelor's degree or higher in music. What every path requires is long-term training and constant practice.
🛤️
Common routes in
School ensembles, lessons, and self-teaching
Many artists start in school choirs, bands, and orchestras, or with private lessons. Others are fully self-taught using online tutorials and home recording software. College music programs, from community college to conservatories, can sharpen skills and build connections, but plenty of successful artists never took that route.
💪
Skills that matter most
Practice, promotion, and persistence
Musical ability is the entry ticket, but the BLS specifically calls out promotional skills: building a fan base through community, word of mouth, and social media. Add discipline (daily practice), resilience (rejection is constant), and basic business sense, and you have the real toolkit.

What Does It Pay?

Artist pay works differently from most jobs. Musicians are usually paid per gig, per project, or per hour of work rather than a yearly salary, so the BLS measures this career in hourly wages. Watch the range fill in below, and notice how wide it is.

What this means: half of working musicians and singers earn more than $42.45 per hour of paid work, and half earn less. But here's the catch: many musicians work part time or have gaps between paid gigs, so total yearly income varies enormously. Many artists also earn from several streams at once: shows, streaming, teaching lessons, session work, and merchandise.

Cool Things to Look Forward To

🎶
Your art, out in the world
There is no feeling quite like hearing a stranger sing words you wrote in your bedroom. Artists get to put pieces of themselves into the world permanently.
✈️
Travel and stages
Touring artists see cities and countries their fans dream about, and every night ends with applause. Even regional artists play new rooms and new crowds constantly.
🚪
No permission needed
This is one of the few careers with no gatekeeper. You can write, record, and release a song this weekend. Your career starts whenever you decide it does.
🤝
A built-in community
Musicians collaborate constantly: co-writers, producers, other bands. Many artists describe the friendships as the best part of the whole career.
06

The AI Angle

How AI Is Changing This Career

AI is becoming the independent artist's assistant. Artists now use AI tools to sketch melody ideas, generate backing tracks to practice with, clean up home recordings, and even design cover art, jobs that used to require hiring other people.

But AI also raises the biggest questions in this career: if AI can generate a song, what makes human artists valuable? The early answer from the industry: connection. Fans follow artists because of their story, their voice, and their realness, which are things AI can't copy. The artists who thrive will likely be the ones who use AI tools to work faster while keeping the human parts, the story and the feeling, at the center.

Key Takeaways

01

Being an artist is three jobs in one: creator, performer, and small business owner.

02

No degree is required for most artist paths, but promotional skills and relentless practice are non-negotiable.

03

The median wage is $42.45 per hour of paid work (BLS, May 2024), but steady work is the real challenge, and most artists build several income streams.
