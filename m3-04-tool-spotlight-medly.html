🔦 Tool Spotlight

Medly: Draw a Note, Make a Song

Most music software assumes you already know music. Medly assumes you do not, and that turns out to be the whole point.

Reading time: 8 min Interactive: 2 live studios Turn your sound on
01

The Blank Page Problem

Here is why most people who want to make music never actually make any: they open a professional program, see forty buttons they do not understand, and quietly close it. The tool assumed they already spoke the language.

Medly's answer to that problem is almost aggressively simple. You do not record. You do not play an instrument. You draw notes on a grid. Pick a sound, tap squares, hear music. That is the whole starting experience.

Rather than describe it, here it is. This grid works the same way Medly's does. Higher squares are higher notes, and time runs left to right. Press Play, then draw.

🎨 The Note Painter

🎹 Try this: tap a few squares anywhere on the grid, then press Play. Then switch instruments and listen to the same melody become a totally different mood.
Sound is generated live in your browser. If you hear nothing, check your volume and make sure this tab is not muted.
Did you notice you could not make it sound bad? That was on purpose. This grid only contains notes from one scale, a family of notes that sound good together. Medly and apps like it use the same trick. Removing the possibility of a "wrong" note is the single biggest reason beginners keep going instead of quitting.

So What Exactly Is Medly?

Medly is a music-making app built by a small Canadian company called Medly Labs. It runs on phones, tablets, and Mac, and it is aimed at exactly the person this course is written for: someone curious about making music who has never done it before.

The features that matter most for a beginner: you draw notes instead of playing them, you can drag in pre-made loops, you can record your own voice or an instrument, and there is a History tool that lets you rewind any mistake. That last one sounds small. It is not. Knowing you cannot permanently ruin anything is what makes people brave enough to experiment.

03

Medly's Real Trick: Sections

Here is the part that separates Medly from a toy. Medly breaks a song into Sections, short chunks of music you build once and then copy, paste, and rearrange to assemble a full song.

This is genuinely how professional songs are structured. An intro, a verse, a chorus, another verse, that chorus again. You are not writing three minutes of music. You are writing about thirty seconds of music and arranging it.

Build a song below. Click section buttons to add them to your timeline, then press Play and listen to your arrangement play through. Watch which instrument layers turn on and off as each section arrives.

04

Is Medly Actually an "AI" Tool?

Time for some honesty, because this is an AI course and telling the difference matters.

Medly is not primarily an AI tool. It does not generate songs for you or master your track with one click. What it has is superb design and enormous libraries of professionally recorded sounds. The reason it feels magical is that a team of humans thought very carefully about how to remove intimidation from the process.

That is a genuinely useful thing to notice. A lot of software gets called "AI-powered" in marketing when the real story is smart design. Being able to ask "what is this tool actually doing?" is one of the most valuable AI skills you can build in this course, and it applies far beyond music.

Which brings us to the tool that does bring the AI to this stage.

The honest recommendation for a beginner: use both. Sketch ideas in Medly on your phone when inspiration hits on the bus. Bring the finished idea into BandLab when you want to collaborate, polish, and master it for release. They are two stages of the same journey, not competitors.

06

Your First Twenty Minutes

Curiosity dies fast without a first step. Here is a concrete one you could do today, for free, in about twenty minutes.

1
Download it and open a blank project. Do not watch a tutorial first. The interface is designed to be figured out, and struggling for four minutes teaches more than a video.
2
Add a drum loop from the library. Do not build drums from scratch yet. A loop gives you a heartbeat to write against, which is far easier than writing into silence.
3
Add one instrument and draw eight notes. Exactly like the Note Painter above. Keep it short. Eight notes that repeat will teach you more than thirty that wander.
4
Copy that section and change one thing. Now you have a verse and a chorus, and you have learned the single most important arranging skill there is: repeat with variation.
5
Export it and send it to one person. This is the step people skip, and it is the one that matters. A finished thirty-second idea that somebody heard beats a perfect song nobody knows exists.
07

Why This Matters for Getting Into the Industry

Think back to the careers you explored earlier in this course. Nearly every one of them, producer, artist, engineer, even marketer, gets easier to enter when you can point at something you made.

Tools like Medly matter because they collapse the distance between "I am curious about music" and "here is a track I made." That used to take a studio, money, and permission. Now it takes an app and an afternoon.

You are not going to make a hit in your first twenty minutes. That is not the point. The point is that the barrier that stopped previous generations, the cost and the intimidation, is mostly gone. What is left is whether you start.

What is Medly's signature way of creating music?
Medly's core idea is that you draw notes rather than perform them, which removes the need to play an instrument before you can create.
QUESTION 2
What are "Sections" in Medly?

AI mastering and live collaboration are BandLab strengths. Medly's strength is composing by drawing notes. Many creators use both, at different stages.
