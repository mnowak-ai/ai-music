03

Hear What Mastering Actually Does

Producers throw around the word mastering constantly, and almost nobody explains it. Here is the plain version: mastering is the final polish on a finished song. It balances the frequencies, evens out the loud and quiet parts, and brings the whole track up to the volume you expect from professional music.

It is the hardest thing in music to explain in writing and the easiest thing to hear. So listen instead. Press play, then flip between the two buttons while the loop runs.

👂 Listen for this: the mastered version is not just louder. The highs are crisper, the low end is tighter, and the quiet parts get lifted closer to the loud parts so nothing disappears.
Why this used to be a huge deal: mastering was once a specialist career of its own. You mailed your finished song to a mastering engineer, waited days, and paid hundreds of dollars. AI mastering tools now do a version of this in under two minutes, and some are completely free. For independent artists, that is an enormous shift.

The Real Versions of What You Just Used

The two studios above are simplified teaching versions. Here are the real platforms doing this at full scale, and two of the three are free to start.

🎹 BandLab
A free browser DAW with AI tools including SongStarter (generates beat ideas), AutoPitch (vocal tuning), and one-click AI mastering, plus live collaboration for up to 50 people on one song.
🎚️ LANDR
Best known for AI mastering: upload a mix and its engine compares it against professional reference tracks, then applies a full mastering chain. Also bundles samples and distribution.
🎧 Soundtrap
A browser-based collaborative DAW owned by Spotify and widely used in schools, so students can record and produce together from different devices.
05

The Part the Machine Cannot Do

The AI Suggest button gave you a competent beat. Competent is the ceiling there, not the floor. It hands you the pattern that statistically works, which is exactly why it can also sound a little generic.

What it could not tell you is whether the beat was right. Whether it fit the story a singer was trying to tell. Whether that slightly strange kick placement you tried was a mistake or the hook. Whether the track should feel joyful, or menacing, or homesick.

That is the deal producers are making with AI right now, and it is a good one: the machine does the hour of technical work in a second, and the human spends that hour on taste.
